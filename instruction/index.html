How to change your location

iPhone with iOS 16 or later · Mac with macOS 12 or later · 20–30 minutes the first time

Connect your iPhone to a Mac, turn on Developer Mode, and set any location you like — system-wide, across every app on the phone. It's done with Xcode, Apple's own developer tool, and it's free.

What you need

  • A Mac with macOS 12 Monterey or later
  • Xcode — free from the Mac App Store, around 10 GB, so start the download first
  • Your iPhone and a USB or Lightning cable
  • A free Apple Account — no paid developer membership needed
  • About 20–30 minutes for the first-time setup; after that it takes a minute

Six steps

  1. Install Xcode

    Get it from the Mac App Store and open it once. On first launch it asks to install additional components — let that finish before you continue.

  2. Connect your iPhone and trust the Mac

    Plug the phone in. It asks Trust This Computer? — tap Trust and enter your passcode. On the Mac, Xcode may spend a few minutes preparing the device the first time.

  3. Turn on Developer Mode

    On the iPhone open Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode, switch it on and restart when prompted. The toggle only appears after the phone has been connected to Xcode at least once.

  4. Run any app on the phone from Xcode

    Xcode can simulate a location only while it is attached to an app running on the device, so it needs a project — any project. File → New → Project → iOS → App is enough: give it a name, pick your Apple Account under Signing & Capabilities, choose your iPhone as the destination and press ⌘R.

    If the iPhone shows an Untrusted Developer message, open Settings → General → VPN & Device Management, tap the certificate and choose Trust, then press ⌘R again.

  5. Get the location file from Pin Share

    On your iPhone, open the pin you want and tap Export GPX file. Send the file to your Mac — AirDrop is quickest. It's named after the place, and that name is what Xcode will show in its menu.

    Xcode has no field for typing coordinates when it's driving a real iPhone; a GPX file is how a custom spot gets in.

  6. Add the file and switch the location on

    Drag the file into the project you just ran, or choose Debug → Simulate Location → Add GPS Exchange to Project… and select it.

    Now open Debug → Simulate Location again — your place sits in the list under the file's name, below Xcode's own cities. Choose it. That's it — every app on your iPhone now sees the new coordinates.

You're somewhere else now

Every app that reads your GPS — Maps, social networks, games, dating apps — now sees the spot you set, not the one you're standing on. Your App Store country and Apple Account region stay exactly as they were.

Keep Xcode connected. The location stays set as long as the debug session is running. Press Stop in Xcode or unplug the cable and your real GPS resumes on its own — so you can switch back any time.
